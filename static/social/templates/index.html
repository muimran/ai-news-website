Social templates · Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn

One calm system for every post

Feed posts, carousels and stories for Second Order, in English and Bangla, light and dark. Photos in full colour, each topic with one quiet tone, and a template for every shape of picture.

One file serves every platform: a 1080 × 1350 feed post goes to Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn as it is, and a 1080 × 1920 story to Instagram and Facebook. Select any image to open it at full size; files are named number, template, mode, variant.

Changed in this round

  • Photos are in full colour. Colour comes only from the website’s two accent families, orange and indigo, paired per topic as the site’s illustrations pair them.
  • Carousels carry a row of dots, one per slide, the current one filled in the topic’s tone (purple on a new story).
  • Pictures are bigger and come in more shapes: edge to edge at the top or bottom, kept whole at any shape, or filling the slide with the words on it.
  • Single posts, templates 7 to 10, are their own bolder family: quote cards, image posts, statements and big numbers.
  • Big numbers are smaller (220 px) and set in the sans, not the serif; they can sit on a solid block of the colour, the way the FT sets them.
  • No slide is only black and white or only grey and black: every one, light or dark, carries its topic’s tone.
  • The stories, templates 11 to 14, are added.

  • Only the two families the website’s illustrations use. Each topic pairs them the same way the site does; the topic number in the notch does the identifying, not the colour.
  • Accent fills: blocks behind a key figure or a big number, the chart’s key bar, timeline marks, the current dot. Orange takes black words (6.1:1), indigo white (5.9:1). Orange is never used as text on Card (2.9:1).
  • Wash is the pale tint: the notch tab, slides with no photo, quote slides, the space round a photo kept whole. Ink on it stays above 12:1.
  • Dark mode: orange stays (5.8:1 on dark Card); indigo moves to its middle tint #9C9BC9 (6.5:1); washes are 13% of the tint on dark Background.
  • Purple stays only for new stories, and always comes with the word New.
  • Every slide carries its tone somewhere: a wash, a number, a heading, a bar, a dot or a label. Black and white alone, or grey and black alone, is never enough.

  • The notch is a solid tab cut into the top-left corner, running from the top edge: the website’s notch at twice its size, without the line across the top. It takes the topic’s wash; on a slide that is already the wash, the page’s grey. Its number box starts where the words start, 72 px in.
  • The notch names the type (Data, Explainer, Investigation). Quote cards carry no notch at all: the quote marks already say what it is. On a NEW post the notch says New and the type sits beside it. Cards carry no dates; the platform shows when a post went up.
  • The same tab on a photo that fills the slide, so the notch looks the same wherever it appears. On a NEW post it turns purple, with white words.
  • Dots: 24 px circles, 16 px apart, bottom-right on every carousel slide, level with the logo. Open circles in Ink; the current slide filled in the topic’s accent, or purple on a new story. On a photo they turn white with the current one in the dark-mode accent. The cover adds an arrow after them. They replace the slide counter.

4 · Images

  • Full colour, as shot. Levels can be corrected; no filters, no tints, no duotone.
  • Any shape fits. A photo can run edge to edge at the top or bottom and take the room the words leave; Fit keeps it whole, at its own shape, on the topic’s wash; or it fills the slide with the words on it.
  • Every stock or illustrative image carries the credit Illustrative image · [Source], 16 px under it, right-aligned, or with the words on a full photo.
  • Screenshots stay as captured, because they are evidence: cropped to the part that matters, a 3 px Ink line round them, credited Screenshot · [Source], [Date].
  • Never AI-made images, robots, circuits or glowing brains.

IIn the gridInstagram 3:4 · Facebook 1:1

The profile grids, mixed

Both apps show a post whole in the feed. Only the profile grids crop it, and they crop differently.

Instagram keeps the middle 3:4, losing 34 px from each side, on phones and on computers. Signed in on a computer it shows four columns instead of three; the tiles are the same shape, just more of them in a row. Every notch, headline and logo starts 72 px in, so all of it survives.

Facebook’s Photos tab keeps the middle square, losing 135 px from the top and the bottom. Every headline, number, quote and photo survives, but the notch (0–132 px) and the logo row (1218–1278 px) fall outside it. That is a trade we chose: moving them inside the square would bring back the empty band above the notch. The page’s own name and picture sit right above that grid anyway.
